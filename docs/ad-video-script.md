# WeredaConnect — AI Video Advertisement Script

## Overview

| Detail | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Duration** | 50–58 seconds (full cut) / 30 seconds (short cut) |
| **Tone** | Warm, hopeful, empowering — never patronizing |
| **Music** | Ethiopian krar/masinko melody building to modern Ethio-jazz fusion |
| **Voiceover** | Amharic (male or female, warm and confident) with English subtitles |
| **Audience** | Ethiopian farmers, truckers, wholesalers, NGO/government stakeholders |
| **Deliverables** | 9:16 (TikTok/Reels), 16:9 (YouTube/presentation), 1:1 (social posts) |

---

## Narrative Arc

```
HOOK       (0–4s)    → Emotional image: farmer alone with harvest, no road out
PROBLEM    (4–14s)   → The daily struggle: no transport, spoiled produce, lost income
TURN       (14–18s)  → Discovery of WeredaConnect
SOLUTION   (18–34s)  → Voice-first, offline, transport matching, fair payment
IMPACT     (34–44s)  → Lives transformed — family, education, community
CTA        (44–52s)  → Download now, tagline, logo
```

This follows the **Hook → Tension → Resolution → Emotion → Action** framework, grounded in real Ethiopian rural life.

### SCENE 1 — Hook: The Farmer's Dilemma (0:00–0:04)

**Visual (AI Video Prompt):**

> Wide aerial drone shot slowly descending over a vast Ethiopian highland teff field at golden hour. A lone farmer in white traditional gabi stands among overflowing sacks of grain, looking down the empty dirt road that stretches to the horizon. No vehicles. No people. Just him and his harvest. Slow camera push-in. Cinematic, warm golden light, shallow depth of field, shot on Arri Alexa, 4K.

**Text Overlay:**

Amharic: `ምርቴ ደርሷል...`
English subtitle: *"My harvest is ready..."*

**Sound:** Silence. Then a single, sustained krar note. Wind in the field.

**Duration:** 4 seconds

**Why this works:** Opens with a universal image — abundance with nowhere to go. The silence and single note create immediate emotional tension.

## Production Workflow

### Step 1: Generate Music (Do First)

**Tool:** [Suno](https://suno.com) or [Udio](https://udio.com)

**Prompt:**

> Ethiopian instrumental music. Start with solo krar (Ethiopian lyre), slow and melancholic, 70 BPM. At 15 seconds, add masinko (single-string fiddle) and transition to hopeful major key. At 30 seconds, build into warm Ethio-jazz fusion — add upright bass, light percussion (kebero drum), and subtle modern elements. Crescendo at 42 seconds. Resolve cleanly at 52 seconds with a final krar strum. No vocals. Cinematic, emotional, inspiring. Total duration: 55 seconds.

Generate 3–4 variations and pick the one with the best emotional arc.

### Step 2: Generate Voiceover

**Tool:** [ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io)

**Approach:**
- Option A: Record a native Amharic speaker and use ElevenLabs for cleanup/enhancement
- Option B: Use ElevenLabs voice cloning with an Amharic voice sample
- Option C: Find an Amharic voice actor on Fiverr ($15–30)

**Full voiceover script (for recording as one take with pauses):**

```
[2s pause]
ወረዳ ኮኔክት — ከእርሻ እስከ ገበያ።
[2s pause]
በአማርኛ ይናገሩ — መጻፍም ኢንተርኔትም አያስፈልግም።
[3s pause]
በአቅራቢያዎ ያለ ሹፌር ያገኛሉ — ፍትሃዊ ዋጋ፣ ቅድሚያ ይታወቃል።
[4s pause]
ገበሬው ሲያተርፍ — ልጆች ይማራሉ፣ ማህበረሰብ ይለወጣል።
[2s pause]
ወረዳ ኮኔክት — አሁን ይጀምሩ።
```

### Step 3: Generate Video Scenes

**Recommended Tools (ranked by quality for African/Ethiopian content):**

| Tool | Strength | Weakness |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Kling 2.0** | Best at realistic human faces and diverse ethnicities | Slower generation |
| **Runway Gen-4 Turbo** | Excellent cinematic quality, good motion | Can struggle with specific ethnic features |
| **Veo 2 (Google)** | Strong landscape and aerial shots | Limited availability |
| **Sora (OpenAI)** | Good cinematic feel | May default to Western aesthetics |
| **Hailuo (MiniMax)** | Fast, decent quality | Less control over details |

**Important — Ethiopian Content Tips for AI Generators:**

1. **Always specify "Ethiopian"** in every prompt — not just "African." AI models tend to default to West African or generic features otherwise.
2. **Reference specific clothing:** "white gabi/netela cloth," "traditional habesha kemis," — not just "traditional clothing."
3. **Reference specific crops:** "teff field," "green khat bushes," "coffee plants" — not generic "farm."
4. **Reference specific vehicles:** "Isuzu truck," "blue minibus taxi" — these are iconic in Ethiopia.
5. **Reference specific landscape:** "Ethiopian highlands," "eucalyptus trees," "tin-roof houses" — not generic "African village."
6. **Run multiple generations** per scene (at least 4) and pick the best. Expect 50% rejection rate for authenticity.
7. **Avoid stereotypes:** No "poverty porn." People should look dignified, capable, and resourceful — because they are.

**Add this suffix to every video generation prompt:**

> `, Ethiopian setting, authentic Ethiopian faces and features, cinematic, 4K, natural lighting, warm color grading, documentary style`

### Step 4: Generate App Screen Recordings

**Tool:** Figma or Flutter demo + screen recording

For Scene 4 (voice UI) and Scene 5 Shot A (map matching), create mockup screen recordings:

1. Build a quick Figma prototype or use the actual Flutter app
2. Record the screen interaction with smooth, deliberate taps
3. Overlay onto the AI-generated phone-in-hand footage using CapCut or After Effects

This looks far more convincing than AI-generating app UI screens.

### Step 5: Assembly & Post-Production

**Tool:** [CapCut](https://capcut.com) (free, excellent for mobile-first editing) or DaVinci Resolve (pro)

**Assembly checklist:**

- [ ] Import all generated video clips, arrange per scene order
- [ ] Lay down the music track first, align scene cuts to beat changes
- [ ] Add voiceover, align to music and visuals
- [ ] Add Amharic text overlays (use **Noto Sans Ethiopic** font — free from Google Fonts)
- [ ] Add English subtitles below Amharic text (smaller, white with dark outline)
- [ ] Add cross-dissolve transitions between scenes (0.3s each, no flashy effects)
- [ ] Color grade for consistency across AI-generated clips (warm, golden tones)
- [ ] Add WeredaConnect logo watermark (small, bottom-right, 20% opacity) throughout
- [ ] Export in 9:16 (1080x1920) for mobile and 16:9 (1920x1080) for web/presentations
- [ ] Final review: watch on a phone screen (not desktop) — that's how the audience sees it

## Quality Checklist Before Publishing

- [ ] All Amharic text is proofread by a native speaker
- [ ] No AI artifacts visible (extra fingers, warped text, floating objects)
- [ ] People look authentically Ethiopian (not generically "African")
- [ ] Phone shown is a budget Android, not an iPhone
- [ ] Audio levels: voiceover is clear over music (music at -12dB, voice at -3dB)
- [ ] Subtitles are readable on a 5-inch phone screen
- [ ] Video works with sound OFF (text tells the story independently)
- [ ] No copyrighted music or images used
- [ ] App screenshots match current app design
- [ ] Tested playback on a low-end phone (compression artifacts check)
