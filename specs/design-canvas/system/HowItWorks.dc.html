How a check-in travels
From the trucker's phone to the buyer's and farmer's map. Rides the existing /sync queue, so it is offline first like every other write in the app.
Trucker phone Server Buyer and farmer Rules
PHONE
GPS fix
Hourly, at each QR scan, every 15 min within 10 km of a drop-off, or when the driver taps.
PHONE
Saved on the phone first
Isar queue, about 120 bytes each. No network: kept and sent later with its real time.
SYNC
POST /sync, batched
entityType location_ping. Idempotency key: trucker, trip and capture time.
SERVER
Checks before storing
The trip's own trucker, a delivery picked up, inside Ethiopia, time not in the future.
BUYER · FARMER
Live trip map
Drawn on the phone from bundled places and roads, no map tiles. Updates every 5 min while open; offline shows the last trail.
API
GET /deliveries/{id}/track
That delivery's buyer and farmer only. Trail from their own pickup, ETA window.
DYNAMODB
trip-locations table
PK tripId, SK capturedAt. Rounded to about 100 m. Expires 30 days after settlement.
RULE
When sharing runs
Starts at the trip's first pickup scan, once the driver agrees. Each check-in carries its accuracy and whether Android flagged it as mocked. Ends by itself when every delivery on the trip is delivered, cancelled or disputed. The driver can pause for a rest stop or overnight, and buyers see Paused. On Android a foreground service with a visible notification does the work, so no all-the-time location permission is needed. Web drivers check in only while the page is open.
RULE
Privacy
Never on trip rows, since every buyer can browse trips. Other stops stay unnamed. Admin sees full trails in disputes.
ON EACH CHECK-IN
Side effects
First check-in 2 km past the pickup marks the delivery in transit. Within 5 km of a drop-off: truck_nearby push to that buyer, once. Over 130 km/h between check-ins, or a mock-location app: fraud signal.
